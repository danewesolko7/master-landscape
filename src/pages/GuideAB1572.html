The short answer

California's AB 1572 bans using potable water to irrigate nonfunctional turf. Commercial, industrial and institutional properties must comply by January 1, 2028. HOA common areas follow on January 1, 2029. Residential lawns are not affected.

Deadlines by property type

Deadline Who must comply
January 1, 2027 Local government, public agency and public water system properties (outside disadvantaged communities)
January 1, 2028 Commercial, industrial and institutional properties
January 1, 2029 HOA common areas, common interest developments and certain community service organizations
January 1, 2031 or later Public properties in disadvantaged communities, or when state conversion funding is available

What counts as nonfunctional turf

Functional turf is turf used for recreation or community gatherings. Everything else is nonfunctional, including turf in street rights-of-way and parking lots, and turf that is fenced off or otherwise can't be used.

You can still use potable water where it's needed to keep trees and other perennial, non-turf plants alive, or to address an immediate health and safety need.

What to do now

  1. 1

    Map your turf

    Mark every turf area as functional (used for recreation or gatherings) or nonfunctional.

  2. 2

    Check rebates first

    Many turf replacement rebates, including MWDOC's, require approval before any turf is removed.

  3. 3

    Plan the conversion

    Design replacement planting and convert irrigation to drip or other efficient systems.

  4. 4

    Schedule before the deadline

    Contractors book up as deadlines approach. Plan the work a season ahead.
