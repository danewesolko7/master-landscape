The short answer

A good HOA landscape contract spells out the scope, the schedule, who supervises, how irrigation and trees are handled, what extra work costs, and how performance is reviewed. Vague contracts lead to disputes.

Scope and schedule

  • Areas covered: a map of common areas, slopes, entries and parkways.
  • Tasks and frequency: mowing, edging, pruning, weeding and cleanup, with how often each happens.
  • Supervision: who is on site and how to reach them.

Irrigation and water

  • Repairs: what is included and what is billed as extra.
  • Scheduling: who sets controllers and how often they change.
  • Water rules: responsibility for water supplier restrictions and AB 1572 planning.

Trees

  • Pruning cycle: which trees, how often and to what standard.
  • Arborist reports: when they are provided and at what cost.
  • Emergencies: response for fallen trees and storm damage.

Extras, reviews and terms

  • Extra work: how it is quoted and approved before it starts.
  • Walkthroughs: regular property reviews with the manager or board.
  • Term and notice: contract length and how either side can end it.
