The short answer

A commercial irrigation audit measures how evenly and efficiently a system waters, finds the leaks and broken parts, and ends with a list of repairs and schedule changes ranked by water saved.

What the auditor checks

  • System condition: leaks, broken or tilted heads, clogged nozzles and pressure problems.
  • Distribution uniformity: how evenly each zone applies water, measured with a catch-can test.
  • Controller schedule: run times compared with plant needs, soil and season.
  • Coverage: dry spots, overspray onto pavement and runoff.

Why "distribution uniformity" matters

When a zone waters unevenly, the schedule is set long enough to keep the driest spot green. Everything else is overwatered. Fixing uniformity lets you shorten run times across the whole zone.

What you get afterward

A written report: zone-by-zone findings, the measured results, recommended repairs ranked by water saved, a revised schedule, and any rebates the upgrades may qualify for.
